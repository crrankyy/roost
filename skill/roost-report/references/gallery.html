Group

Page shell

Every report uses all six, in this order, unchanged.

01 · Shell

Top bar

The report's name on the left, the theme button on the right. The scope · date · version line lives in the hero (.hero-meta), so nothing is lost when a host replaces this bar.

  • No links and it doesn't stick to the top. The contents rail is the navigation.
  • Always data-slot="host-bar": inside Roost the page sets data-host and Roost's own bar takes this one's place.
  • The wordmark is the <title>, word for word.
02 · Shell

Hero

Label, the page's only h1, a lead paragraph, the meta line (.hero-meta: scope · date · version), fact pills. Three variants: .hero.media with a code window or figure on the right (shown), solo, and solo with a stat row (02b).

  • The headline is a claim or a plain title, not a question.
  • The media variant stacks below 1024px.
02b · Shell

Hero with stats

The solo hero with a stat row under the copy, inside the hero's .wrap. For reports whose headline is a handful of numbers.

  • Use this or a stat row in the first section, not both.
  • Same stat-row rules: 3 to 6 numbers, each one carrying something.
03 · Shell

Contents rail

Numbered sections in a sticky rail beside the reading column. report.js highlights the section in view.

  • Left out when a report has fewer than 4 sections: use .layout.no-toc.
  • Moves above the content below 1024px.
04 · Shell

Section

Rule, "NN · TOPIC" label, serif h2, intro. The only way to start a top-level section.

  • Write only the topic in the label (<span class="kicker">Limits</span>); the number comes from the section's position, so it always matches the contents rail. .kicker.manual keeps a number you type, such as an appendix "A".
  • The id is what the contents rail links to.
  • The intro is one or two sentences; content blocks follow 24px apart.
05 · Shell

Sub-section

A serif h3 and its content, inside a section.

  • There are no h4 headings. Smaller groupings use a card, a note or a detail card.
06 · Shell

Footer

Where the data came from, when the report was built, and its caveats.

  • Always dark. Always says where the data came from.
Group

Text

Paragraphs stop at 68 characters; blocks run the full column.

07 · Text

Prose

Paragraphs, bullet lists, inline code, links, bold lead-ins.

  • Bold opening phrase, then the sentence: "One story, one session. …".
  • Inline code for names a reader might search for, not for emphasis.
08 · Text

Numbered steps

Procedures, workflows, reproduction and remediation steps.

  • Each step: a short title, then what happens.
  • Order matters; if it doesn't, use a bullet list.
09 · Text

Quote

A record's own words: a message, an error, a tool result. .machine for machine text.

  • The caption says who, when and where.
  • Quote word for word; say so if anything was substituted.
  • Line breaks inside a quote need <br>; the source's own line breaks are ignored.
10 · Text

Definition list

Glossary, field reference.

  • Terms in sentence case. Definitions one or two sentences.
  • Stacks on narrow screens.
Group

Summary and emphasis

What a reader takes away if they read nothing else.

11 · Summary

Stat row

3 to 6 headline numbers, with an optional change and sparkline.

  • No stat that carries nothing ("Sessions: 1").
  • Labels are one line; numbers share a baseline.
12 · Summary

Key findings

The 3 to 5 sentences someone reads if they read nothing else. The first thing in <main>, before the first section.

  • Bold opening phrase, one sentence after it.
  • Each finding ends with a link to the section that proves it: "; see the five runs".
  • No frame: its place straight after the hero says what it is.
13 · Summary

Verdict

One decision or recommendation, with a confidence level. Add good, warning, serious or critical.

  • The call is two or three words; the reason is one sentence.
  • Colour never alone: the word carries it.
14 · Summary

Callout

The single most important point in the report. Add a severity class to colour it: .good, .warning, .serious, .critical; no class gives the neutral accent.

  • At most one per report. If two things feel this important, neither is.
  • Severity matches the finding: a lost handoff is .critical, a slow but correct run .warning, a clean result .good. No severity, no class.
15 · Summary

Note

Context, a rule of thumb, "how this relates to familiar practice".

  • Several per report is fine. Keep it under 80 words.
  • A fill and no border, so it stays quieter than cards.
16 · Summary

Status aside

A warning, risk, caveat or assumption: a coloured rule on the left, a status dot and a word. Give the aside the same status class as its dot.

  • Status colours mean good to critical only; never a decoration.
  • No frame: it reads as a margin note attached to the text above it.
17 · Summary

Illustration box

Anything not from the record: a hypothetical, a made-up example.

  • Required whenever content isn't real. The label is fixed text.
Group

Cards

Parallel ideas side by side. Grids drop columns instead of squeezing cards.

18 · Cards

Card grid

Parallel ideas: .grid for 3, .grid.two for 2 or 4 (also two quotes side by side). Filled cards for a few weighty ones; .outline for many light ones.

  • Card count must suit the column count: no lone card on a row.
  • 3 → 2 → 1 columns.
19 · Cards

Stage cards

3 to 5 stages in order.

  • Mono "stage N · name" label, a short title, one line.
20 · Cards

Option comparison

Options side by side with pros and cons.

  • At most one .recommended. Keep the lists the same length so cards align.
21 · Cards

Finding card

Audit, review and bug findings: severity, title, where, evidence, fix.

  • Sorted by severity, most severe first.
  • Evidence is a quote or a record, never a paraphrase.
22 · Cards

Detail card

One entity in depth: a run, an incident, a service.

  • Header label (.pill.label, never the accent colour), h3, a strip of facts, then labelled parts.
  • Long records inside it are collapsible record windows.
Group

Data

Tables, states and kinds. Charts follow in their own group.

23 · Data

Table

Reference rows and comparisons. .num right-aligns numbers; a tfoot row holds totals.

  • Scrolls sideways inside its own box, never the page.
  • Long values go on their own line, not wrapped mid-chip. IDs and dates never break at a hyphen: report.js wraps them on its own.
  • A table is a centred card with a title, a footer, or both: <caption> as the first child of the table is the title; <p class="source"> inside .table-wrap, after the table, is the footer. check.py fails a table with neither.
  • Under any other block (figure, list), the source goes straight after it. Charts use their spec's source.
23b · Data

Table colour

Three ways to colour a table; use one per table. <th class="num heat"> tints that column's cells by value, light to strong, one scale across all heat columns of the table. <span class="change">+22%</span> gets an arrow and a pill from its sign, anywhere. <th data-slot="1"> puts chart colour 1 beside a header, to match a chart above.

  • Heat only columns of the same measure (retention by month, not retention and headcount).
  • The colour repeats the number, never replaces it; the text stays ink.
  • No zebra stripes or coloured number text on top.
24 · Data

Status

Yes/no/partly, pass/fail, done/open: a dot and a word.

  • Four reserved meanings: good, warning, serious, critical. No class means neutral.
  • Always a word next to the dot.
25 · Data

Kind tag

Classifying rows: lesson, risk, assumption, debt, bug, question. Six kinds, .k1 to .k6, assigned in order of first use.

  • A kind keeps its number everywhere in the report. Kinds have square markers; round dots are status.
  • Show the legend once, where the tags first appear.
  • A chart that plots the same kinds sets each series' "slot" to its tag number, so Risk is the same colour in both.
27 · Data

Timeline log

Dated events: releases, incident timelines, run-by-run summaries.

  • The label column holds a time, a version or a run; newest first for releases, oldest first for incidents.
Group · component 26

Charts

A <figure class="chart"> holding a JSON spec; charts.js draws it. Every chart gets a legend for 2+ series, tooltips on hover and keyboard focus, and a "Show data" table.

26a · Chart

Column

Comparing a few categories; grouped columns for several series per category.

  • Values on the caps for a single series.
  • Many or long category names: use a bar chart.
26b · Chart

Bar

Ranked quantities, long category names. "reference": {"value", "label"} draws a dashed budget or target line, on bar and column.

  • Sort largest first unless the categories have a natural order.
  • Keep category names under about 24 characters; longer ones are cut with "…" on phones. "format": {"trim": true} drops trailing zeros.
26c · Chart

Stacked and 100%

What a total is made of ("stacked": true), or shares that each sum to 100% ("normalize": true). Works on bar and column.

  • For one whole at a glance, use the segmented bar instead.
26d · Chart

Line

Trends over time. Up to 4 series are labelled at their ends; the crosshair reads every series at once.

  • Starts at zero unless "zero": false.
  • One story series and the rest context: "emphasis": "Web".
26e · Chart

Area

Volume over time; "stacked": true for what the volume is made of.

  • The fill is a 10% wash; the edge line carries the value.
26f · Chart

Sparkline

A small trend inside text, a table cell or a stat. Muted line, last point in the accent.

  • No axes, so always beside a number that states the value.
26g · Chart

Scatter

How two measures relate. Points as [x, y, "label", highlight].

  • At most 3 series; past that, colours stop separating for everyone. Fold the rest into "Other".
  • Label only highlighted points.
26h · Chart

Histogram

How values are spread. Give raw values and the script bins them, or give bins.

  • Bins touch with a 2px gap; one colour.
26i · Chart

Heatmap

A grid of values. Sequential (one hue, 7 classes) by default; "diverging": true for change around a midpoint.

  • Values print inside cells only where they fit.
  • Empty cells (null) read as "no data".
26j · Chart

Gantt

Durations on a timeline: runs, phases, jobs. ISO times give a time axis (UTC); numbers give a numeric one.

  • Colour by group only when the group means something; otherwise one colour.
26k · Chart

Meter list

Progress against a limit or target, one row each. status colours a row and adds the word; statusLabel replaces the word ("Miss", "Done"). target draws a tick with its own tooltip.

  • Figures are always printed; the bar only shows the share.
  • max is both the scale and the "/ max" figure; "showMax": false hides the figure.
26l · Chart

Segmented bar

One whole split into 6 parts or fewer, at a glance. "thin": true for an 8px bar beside figures.

  • Shares print inside parts only where they fit; the legend names every part.
Group

Code and records

Code scrolls sideways; records people read wrap.

28 · Code

Code window

Source code, config, file trees, terminal output.

  • Never wraps; scrolls sideways.
  • Always highlighted. Code: <code class="language-rust"> and prism.js colours it (typescript, javascript, rust, toml, json, bash, python, yaml, markdown, sql, go, diff, markup). Plain-text excerpts: tint by hand, .k for field labels and names, .s strings, .c arrows and asides.
29 · Code

Record window

JSON, logs, prompts, transcripts: text people read. Wraps. Collapsible with <details>; .ln.new marks lines added since the previous record.

  • Records over 30 lines start closed, with their size in the bar.
  • Put each line in a .ln span when marking lines; the spans may sit on separate source lines.
30 · Code

Diff window

Before and after a change, one line per .ln: .add, .del, or plain context.

  • Keep three lines of context around each change at most.
  • Never wraps: long lines scroll sideways. Tint code by hand inside the lines (.k, .s, .c).
  • From a unified diff, drop the leading +/-: .add and .del draw their own.
31 · Code

Side-by-side pair

Two panes compared directly: stored vs given, before vs after.

  • Each pane is a record or a diff window, never a code window; a diff scrolls inside its pane.
  • Stacks below 900px.
Group

Diagrams

Pills and boxes for simple shapes; an SVG figure for anything else.

32 · Diagram

State strip

A lifecycle or a set of phases, with what each state requires.

  • The final state is dark. Exceptions go in side-state pills below.
  • Becomes a vertical list on phones.
33 · Diagram

Flow

A pipeline or data path in a light frame. .store marks a place data rests. .k1–.k6 on a step colours it by kind (here: Claude's work, the database, the stream).

  • Linear only, in one row: at most 6 steps, 5 reads better. Branches, loops or more: use a figure.
  • Stacks vertically on phones.
34 · Diagram

Figure

Any custom diagram as inline SVG, drawn with the report's classes (.box, .box-dark, .edge, .edge.dashed for async or optional, .arrow, text.muted for secondary labels) so both themes work.

  • No hex colours inside the SVG. A caption says what to look at.
  • Colour by kind: .k1–.k6 on a .box tints it and outlines it, on an .edge colours the line and its arrowhead. Same kind, same colour; leave plain what has no kind.
  • Stays at least 560px wide and scrolls on phones so labels stay readable; a small diagram can lower it with style="--figure-min: 320px".
Group

Inline pieces

Styles rather than components, fixed by the stylesheet.

34b · Diagram

Screenshot

A framed <img> with a caption: <figure class="figure shot">. For screenshots and other raster images.

  • Alt text says what the image shows; the caption says what to look at.
  • Crop to the part that matters. Embed as a data URI (PNG or WebP, at most 1600px wide) so the report stays one file.
Inline

Pills, tags, keys, code, links

.pill for facts and side states, .pill.dark for a final state, .pill.label for an identity label ("Run 2"), .pill.accent for one highlight per card, .since for version markers, .nowrap to keep anything else on one line (IDs and dates are kept together automatically).
